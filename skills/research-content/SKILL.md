# research-content (Skill 0 — the first step)

**You can't write until you know what you're writing about.** This is the front of
the pipeline:

**idea bank → `research-content` (THIS) → `write-content` → `blotato-post` → `cadence`**

Take one idea, research it for real, hand `write-content` a brief with substance —
so the draft stands on facts and a sharp angle, not air. **Research only. Never
drafts the post, never posts.**

---

## Inputs

- **An idea.** Either:
  - **Pick one** from the idea bank at `~/Desktop/Social Media Posts: Ideas/`
    (`00-INDEX.md` lists the `ideas-*.md` files; graduates into
    `ai-social-system/content-db/` over time), **or**
  - **a given idea / topic** Brent hands over directly.
- **Optional pillar + platform** (else inferred). The four pillars (from the idea
  bank's `00-INDEX.md`): **Inform** your team · **Train** your team · **Make your
  team more effective** · **Save your team money/time.**

## The frame (every brief serves it)

**"What will AI / Tiger do for YOUR TEAM?"** Audience = team-first LEADERS (direct
sales / affiliate / network marketing); repel the me-first individual. Voice =
biography, never earnings. Standard = **value must exceed cost.**

---

## Process

1. **Lock the idea.** If picking from the bank, read `00-INDEX.md` then the relevant
   `ideas-*.md`, choose one, and state it in one line. Name the **pillar** and the
   **team-first angle** before researching.

2. **Research it thoroughly.** Don't write from memory — go find the substance:
   - **What's true now** — `WebSearch` for current facts, numbers, named tools,
     dates. Prefer primary sources.
   - **What people are actually saying** — the **`last30days`** skill (Reddit / X /
     YouTube / HN) for real language, objections, and fears around the topic.
   - **The proof** — concrete stats, a named example, a dated event. Capture exact
     figures and where they came from.
   - **Brent's lived angle** — connect the topic to his story / the thesis (build
     your own AI-powered distribution org), so the post is his, not generic.

3. **Write the brief** in the fixed format below (`references/brief-template.md`).

4. **Hand off** to `write-content` with the brief as its source material.

---

## Output — the research brief (this exact structure)

A brief `write-content` can draft straight from. Full template +
example: `references/brief-template.md`. The sections:

- **The idea (one line)** + **pillar** + **team-first angle**
- **What this actually is** — plain English, ≤150 words, no jargon
- **Why it matters right now** — 3 reasons, each with a real stat or example
- **What most people get wrong** — the counterintuitive angle = **the hook**
- **Three key points** — each: one claim · one concrete example · one takeaway
- **Brent's angle** — how it ties to his story / the thesis (specific, not generic)
- **Sources** — real URLs / data points only. **List them; never invent one.**

---

## Compliance (research is where bad claims get caught early)

- **Real sources only.** Every stat is traceable to a real URL or named source. If
  you can't source it, cut it — **never fabricate a number, study, or quote.**
- **No income / earnings / guarantee / health claims**, and nothing that implies
  them (see `../write-content/references/never-say.md`). Brent's record =
  biography only, never an income claim.
- Flag anything compliance-risky in the brief so `write-content` never has to guess.

## Out of scope

- **Does not draft the post.** Voice, hooks, CTAs, platform formatting belong to
  `write-content`. This produces the brief; `write-content` turns it into copy.
- **Does not post or schedule** (that's `blotato-post` / `cadence`).
- **Does not pick visuals or thumbnails** (`blotato-post` / `make-thumbnail`).
